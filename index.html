About

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Environmental Science and Engineering at SUSTech in Shenzhen, China, working with Prof. Guomao Zheng. I study the chemical exposome: all the chemicals people are exposed to in daily life, and how they show up in the body.

My work uses high-resolution mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS) to find and measure these chemicals in silicone wristbands, blood and urine. I also build pseudo-targeted methods that turn high-resolution discoveries into fast, routine triple-quadrupole (MRM) measurements. I process and visualise the data in Python.

Originally from Bangladesh, I have studied and lived in China since 2016, training in pharmacy and microbiology before moving into environmental chemistry.

Research

Wearable exposure sampling

Using silicone wristbands as personal passive samplers to measure what people are exposed to, including targeted quantification of 73 chemicals (QACs, OPEs, PFAS, pesticides, phenols, antimicrobials) plus suspect screening.

Pseudo-targeted exposomics

Translating high-resolution MS features into scheduled MRM methods for triple-quadrupole instruments, so wide chemical coverage becomes fast, sensitive and transferable between labs.

Human biomonitoring

Suspect and non-target screening across environmental samples, blood and urine, with careful QA/QC and confidence-level annotation of hundreds of detected chemical features.

Earlier work: airborne fungi

During my MSc at Tianjin University, I ran a year-long air-sampling campaign, identified fungal species, and tested their pathogenicity and medicinal mushroom effects in fruit flies.
